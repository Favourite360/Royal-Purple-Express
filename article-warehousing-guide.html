Bonded vs general warehousing: which does your cargo need?

The right storage decision can defer duty, protect stock and smooth your cash flow. The wrong one quietly adds cost to every shipment. Here is the difference in plain terms.

BlogJune 17, 2026Royal Purple Express Desk

What bonded warehousing actually is

A bonded warehouse holds imported cargo under customs control before duty is paid. Your goods land, clear the port and sit in secured storage, and the duty falls due only when you withdraw them. For importers bringing volume, that is a cash flow instrument: you pay duty as you sell, not all at once on arrival.

What general warehousing is for

General warehousing holds duty-paid or locally sourced stock. It is the working buffer of your supply chain: replenishment stock close to your market, inputs staged for production, orders picked and packed for distribution. No customs formalities, maximum flexibility.

How to choose

  • Importing volume you will sell over months? Bonded storage defers duty and protects margin.
  • Fast-turning stock feeding shops or production? General warehousing close to demand.
  • Uncertain demand? Bonded first, withdraw and pay duty as orders firm up.
  • Export consolidation? General warehousing near the port, staged for stuffing.

The inventory question

Whichever you choose, storage without accurate counts is a liability. Stock you cannot see is stock you reorder twice or run out of once. Our warehousing, bonded and general, comes with inventory management as standard: you always know what is on hand and what to reorder, and pick-and-pack means stock leaves ready for its next stop.
